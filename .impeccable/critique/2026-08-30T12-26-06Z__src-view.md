## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment**: authored, not category-interchangeable. Nothing here reads as a generic kanban template: the type badge ladder, the parent breadcrumb on every card, the rollup fraction beside a hairline bar, the counted shelf that is also the un-place target, and the context row drawn in italic with no controls are all shapes that only this product needs. The commitment to Obsidian's tokens is total and pays off — in light theme it is indistinguishable from a first-party view.

Where specificity is lost is in the *default* state. The tree's resting appearance is a grid of grey placeholder chips, and a stranger's first screen is mostly the word "Assignee" repeated.

**Deterministic scan**: `detect.mjs` returns 3 findings, all in `styles/`, none in `src/` or the rendered DOM.
- `styles/barProgress.css:19` and `:61` — `border-radius: 2px` outside the DESIGN.md `rounded` scale (advisory). Real drift: DESIGN.md declares `bar: 3px` and the CSS uses 2px on a 4px-tall bar. One of the two is stale.
- `styles/release.css:151` — `border-inline-start: 3px solid` flagged as a side-tab accent. **False positive**: the border is `transparent` by default and reserved so the overdue state costs no reflow. The comment above it says exactly that.

Instrument note: the scan reports clean on `src/` and on the rendered DOM, which is trustworthy for CSS token drift and not much else here — a synthetic file full of gradients and blur also came back empty, so treat the DOM pass as unproven rather than as a clean bill.

**Visual overlays**: not available. No display and no interactive browser to present; the live-server + injection flow would produce an overlay nobody can look at. Screenshots and a computed-style probe were used instead.

## Overall Impression

This is a serious piece of work. The interaction model is better than most commercial trackers — every move has a drag, a key and a menu path, every write can be taken back, and the keyboard model (one tab stop for the grid, roving inside it) is correct in a way that most kanban UIs never get to.

The single biggest opportunity: **the view spends its visual budget on what is absent.** Seven "unset" chips per row and one per card mean the eye's first job on every screen is to filter out grey. Nothing is wrong with the information design underneath it; it is buried under a permanent scaffold.

## What's Working

- **The write-safety model is visible in the UI, not just in the code.** A context row is italic, muted, and simply has no chips to click. The rule and its affordance are the same thing — a user never learns the restriction from an error.
- **The board's status density is exactly right.** `5 / 2 ⚠` on Active, `Done 4` on a collapsed rail, a count on every column header, the parent breadcrumb under every card title. Everything a standup needs is on one screen with no hover.
- **Obsidian-native to the pixel.** Every colour measured resolves to an Obsidian token. The light and dark screenshots are the same design, not a design and its dark hack.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] The placeholder chips are the loudest thing on screen, and they fail contrast**
- **Why it matters**: On the default tree, 7 of 9 columns on most rows show a grey chip that means "nothing here" — measured at **2.57:1 in dark and 2.12:1 in light** (12px text, AA needs 4.5:1). They are also interactive: clicking one sets the property. So the noisiest element on screen is simultaneously unreadable and clickable, which is the worst combination — invisible to a low-vision user, distracting to everyone else. On the board and roadmap the same thing puts "Assignee" on every single card.
- **Fix**: Do not draw an unset chip at rest. Show it on row hover / card focus, where it becomes an affordance instead of a texture. If it must persist, raise it to at least 4.5:1 (`--text-muted`, not `--text-faint`) and drop it to an icon-only stub so it stops competing with real values. A row that has a status and two dates should read as three facts, not as two facts and five absences.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable distill`

**[P2] The timeline legend promises a colour mapping it cannot deliver**
- **Why it matters**: Measured from the rendered legend — `Ready`, `Active` and `Concept` are all exactly `rgb(2, 122, 255)`, and `Done` and `Published` are both `rgb(68, 207, 110)`. Five legend entries, three colours. A blue bar on the timeline is unreadable: it is one of three states and the legend cannot tell you which. This is the one projection whose entire job is at-a-glance state, and it is the one that cannot answer.
- **Fix**: Either assign distinct hues to every state in the legend's vocabulary, or stop drawing a legend entry for a colour that duplicates one already spent. The second is honest and cheaper: a legend that lists only what it can distinguish.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable colorize`

**[P3] The selected projection tab is the least readable label in the toolbar**
- **Why it matters**: The active tab's accent text on its 10%-accent tint measures **2.56:1 in dark and 1.65:1 in light**. Every inactive tab beside it measures 6.69-8.13:1. The one control that answers "where am I" is the one the user can least read, and light theme is the worse of the two.
- **Fix**: Keep the accent tint as the selected surface, but set the label to `--text-normal` and carry the state on the tint and the underline. Accent-on-accent-tint never clears 3:1.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable polish`

**[P3] The toolbar is a wall of unlabelled icons**
- **Why it matters**: 16 icon-only buttons on the tree, **21 on the dated roadmap**, all aria-labelled and none visibly labelled. Past the projection switcher there is no grouping, no separator that means anything, and no visual weight difference between "Undo last change" and "Compact rows". A first-timer has 21 identical grey glyphs and must hover each one. Cognitive-load checklist: fails *minimal choices* (>4 at one decision point), fails *chunking*, fails *visual hierarchy*.
- **Fix**: Three groups with real separators — projection, create, view-state — and push the rest behind the "More toolbar actions" overflow that already exists. The dated roadmap adding five more buttons inline rather than into that menu is the tell that the overflow is under-used.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable layout`

**[P3] The roadmap's real content is the shelf, and the shelf is a one-line band**
- **Why it matters**: On the shipped fixture, **20 of 32 items are unplaced on the horizon axis and 21 of 32 on the dated axis**. The buckets get the full canvas; two-thirds of the backlog gets a collapsed 30px strip. On the dated axis this is stark: two bars and one diamond in a 1200px chart, with a mostly-empty year of grid, over a band holding 21 items. The shelf also moves — top on horizons, bottom on dates — so the one element that holds most of the data is also the one that relocates between axes.
- **Fix**: Pick one position for the shelf and keep it across both axes. Then let the empty roadmap say what it means: an axis with 2 placed items and 21 unplaced ones should open with the shelf expanded, not collapsed. The empty state is not "no data", it is "nothing has been scheduled yet" — which is an invitation to place something.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable onboard`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (First-Timer)** — a stranger installing this into an unknown vault, which PRODUCT.md names as a real audience:
- Lands on a tree where the most repeated word on screen is "Assignee", seven times, in grey. Reads it as broken or as a loading state.
- 16 unlabelled glyphs in the toolbar; the only labelled control is the projection switcher. The one help affordance is labelled "What is happening", which reads as an error message, not as help.
- Switches to Roadmap and sees three buckets holding 12 of 32 items with no explanation of where the other 20 went; the "Unplaced 20" band is one grey line the same weight as a column header.

**Alex (Power User)** — a team member who lives in this view:
- Well served. Alt+arrow moves, roving tabindex, quick filter, undo, three paths per action. Nothing here forces a mouse.
- One friction point, measured: 7 column-resize grips sit in the tab order between the toolbar and the tree grid, so reaching the content by keyboard costs 7 tabs through separators. Defensible (the repo states the rule deliberately) but it is the one place the keyboard model taxes the person it was built for.
- Every property chip is **22px tall** against WCAG 2.2's 24px minimum target. On desktop with a mouse, fine. On the touch path, `row-touch` grows the row to 40px but the chip inside it stays 22px, so the touch affordance does not reach the thing being tapped.

## Minor Observations

- **The title column has no header.** Every other column is labelled (`STATUS`, `HORIZON`, …); the widest and most important one is blank.
- **The POINTS column in the test catalog draws nothing when empty** while every other empty column draws a ghost chip. Whichever behaviour is right, it is not both.
- **Column headers measure 2.97:1 dark / 2.30:1 light** at 12px. Below AA for the labels that name what each column is.
- **The context row title ("Retired platform") measures 2.97 / 2.30.** The muting is intentional and correct in meaning; the value is one step too faint to read.
- **"Done" state text in light theme measures 2.41:1** — the green that carries the most meaning is the least readable colour in the light scheme (it is 7.30:1 in dark).
- **Empty board columns are silent.** The Deliverables board's `Concept 0` column is 700px of nothing with no drop hint.
- **Tag chips clip mid-word** on a 260px board column (`#nee…`), while the same card on the wider roadmap shows `#needs-design` in full.
- **"Clicking a row folds it"** is a statement, not a control label. A toggle's label should name the action or the state, not describe the world.
- **DESIGN.md `rounded.bar: 3px` vs `barProgress.css` 2px** — one of the two is stale.

## Questions to Consider

- What if the tree drew only what a note *has*? A row with a status and two dates would be three chips, not eight. What is actually lost — and could hover recover it?
- The roadmap's shelf holds two-thirds of the backlog. Is the shelf the exception, or is it the main view with three optimistic columns attached?
- If the timeline cannot give five states five colours, should it draw state at all — or should the bar carry progress and let colour mean one thing only?
- What would this look like with a five-button toolbar?
